# Commercial performance review

Source: Chen, D. (2015), UCI Online Retail, DOI 10.24432/C5BW33, CC BY 4.0. Historical UK-based online retailer with international customers. Amounts are GBP. Data contain transactions, not product costs or collections.

The 541,909 source rows include 10,147 rows belonging to exact-duplicate groups. They are retained and flagged because repeated invoice lines may be legitimate. Missing customer IDs remain in company totals. Quarantined rows (2) include negative prices and contradictory cancellation signs; their amount is separately reconciled. Non-product charges and adjustments remain distinct from merchandise sales.

Net classified revenue is GBP 9,769,872.05. SQL monthly totals differ from the classified ledger by GBP 0.00000000. Unknown customer IDs account for 14.7% of gross product sales. The top ten known customers contribute 17.4% of identified gross sales; this is not their share of all revenue.

The bridge from 2011-10 to 2011-11 explains GBP 391,051.58 of net movement with a reconciliation difference of GBP 0.00000000. Shared-product gross sales are decomposed in the documented volume→mix→price order; new/discontinued products, credits and non-product adjustments are separate. Average realized prices reflect discounts and customer mix as well as possible list-price changes. No causal elasticity is inferred.

The final observed month (2011-12) is partial and is excluded from the complete-month trend chart. Cohorts describe observed repeat purchasing, with left-censoring at dataset start and incomplete follow-up for recent cohorts. Seasonality and the short observation window constrain growth conclusions.

Commercial lead: review the top ten known accounts (17.4% of identified gross sales); track concentration monthly. Unknown IDs prevent a complete customer view.

Operations lead: investigate the largest credited product values and match credits to original orders before attributing them to product quality; track credited value and reason codes.

Finance lead: obtain actual landed product and fulfilment costs before recommending price changes; use the separate cost scenarios to identify conclusions sensitive to margin assumptions.

The formula-driven Excel model separates observed revenue from assumed costs. Cost scenarios are hypothetical contributions, not observed profit. The accompanying five-slide presentation contains evidence, decisions and limitations.
